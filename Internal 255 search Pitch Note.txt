Internal Pitch Note
Multi-Observable Phase Selection Test for the 255° Candidate Basin
Purpose

This note outlines a possible experimental-surrogate route for testing whether a preferred phase near 255° can be selected by simultaneous consistency across multiple observables, even if a closed-form first-principles derivation remains incomplete.

The aim is not to claim that 255° has already been proven.
The aim is to define a structured phase-selection stress test that could, in principle, be explored numerically, in analogue models, and eventually in laboratory systems.

The core idea is simple:

If a broad scan over candidate phase angles shows that multiple otherwise distinct observables all become best-behaved inside one narrow basin near 255°, then that angle gains support as a closure-selected attractor surrogate rather than an arbitrarily chosen parameter.

This would not be a theorem.
But it would be a serious and falsifiable multi-observable closure-selection result.

2. Target Basin Idea

The working hypothesis is not that one observable alone selects the preferred phase.
Rather, the hypothesis is that there exists a narrow admissible basin in angle space where multiple independent diagnostics all become simultaneously well-behaved.

This means the target is not simply “the best angle” under one metric.
The target is:

a narrow basin rather than a broad plateau,
showing coincidence across multiple observables,
with enough sharpness to distinguish it from neighbouring angles,
and enough robustness to survive modest changes in weighting or modelling assumptions.

In this framework, 255° would be favoured not because it is imposed, but because it is the only region in which a wide set of closure conditions can be jointly satisfied.

3. Observable Set

The current candidate observable set is:

RG dressing quality
Relic width stability
Overlap hierarchy
CP proxy phase
Current anisotropy
Chemistry smoothness metrics

These are intended to represent structurally different sectors of the framework rather than repeated versions of the same diagnostic.

4. What Each Observable Is Meant to Indicate
4.1 RG dressing quality

This is meant to indicate whether the candidate phase remains well-behaved under scale flow or coarse-graining.

Questions:

Does the phase remain stable under dressing?
Does it behave like a true attractor rather than a fragile local minimum?
Do effective couplings remain controlled near this angle?

A strong result here would support the idea that the candidate phase is not only locally selected, but also scale-stable.

4.2 Relic width stability

This is meant to indicate whether the candidate angle produces a narrow and stable residual basin rather than a diffuse or unstable remnant.

Questions:

Is the basin width well-defined?
Does the width remain stable under perturbation?
Does the candidate avoid runaway broadening or collapse?

A strong result would suggest that the phase produces a physically meaningful remnant structure rather than a numerically accidental point.

4.3 Overlap hierarchy

This is meant to indicate whether the angle generates a well-ordered hierarchy of overlap or coupling strengths, rather than a confused or inverted structure.

Questions:

Do expected ordering relations hold?
Is the hierarchy smooth rather than erratic?
Does the angle preserve the intended dominance structure?

A strong result here would support internal structural consistency.

4.4 CP proxy phase

This is meant to track whether the candidate angle aligns with the intended direction and magnitude of effective asymmetry in the model.

Questions:

Does the phase generate the correct sign of asymmetry?
Is the proxy consistent with the intended chiral structure?
Does it remain smooth across nearby angles?

A strong result would support the idea that the candidate phase is linked to coherent asymmetry rather than arbitrary displacement.

4.5 Current anisotropy

This is meant to indicate whether directional transport or response behaves in a stable and interpretable way near the candidate angle.

Questions:

Is anisotropy controlled and non-pathological?
Does the angle produce a preferred directional structure without instability?
Is the effect measurable in analogue systems?

A strong result would make the candidate phase more physically accessible.

4.6 Chemistry smoothness metrics

This is meant to indicate whether the candidate angle produces smooth rather than jagged or discontinuous behaviour in chemistry-linked structural metrics.

Questions:

Are the associated chemical or overlap-like response functions smooth?
Does the angle avoid abrupt pathological transitions?
Does it favour stable structured behaviour?

A strong result here would help bridge the closure story into chemistry / materials analogues.

5. What Success Would Look Like

Success would require more than “255° scores well.”

It would require:

A. Narrowness

A clear and limited basin, not a broad vague region.

B. Coincidence

Multiple observables peaking, minimising, stabilising, or smoothing in nearly the same angular neighbourhood.

C. Sharpness

A basin that is visibly more structured than nearby alternatives.

D. Robustness

The result should survive:

modest weighting changes,
scan-resolution changes,
reasonable modelling perturbations,
and leave-one-out checks in which one observable is temporarily removed.
E. Transparency

The result should be visible both:

in individual observable plots,
and in a composite closure score.

The strongest case would be a narrow basin near 255° that remains preferred even when one observable at a time is removed from the composite.

7. Candidate Analogue Systems

The eventual experimental route would likely require a physical system that supports:

phase selection,
local stiffness or rigidity,
directional bias,
measurable anisotropy,
and scale-dependent stability.

Possible analogue classes include:

7.2 Nonlinear metamaterials

Useful for:

anisotropic response,
engineered boundary conditions,
and directional stability tests.
7.3 Photonic or waveguide systems

Useful for:

phase transport,

and mode-shaping.
7.4 Condensed-matter phase systems

Useful for:

stiffness analogues,

7.5 Structured chemical or reaction-diffusion systems

Potentially useful for:

smoothness metrics,

The first goal would not be to reproduce the full theory in the lab, but to identify a surrogate system in which angle-dependent multi-observable phase selection can be measured at all.

8. Why This Is Worth Testing

This programme offers a middle path between:

claiming a complete first-principles derivation too early,
and abandoning the 255° question as purely formal.

The proposed scan does not prove the angle by elegance alone.
Instead, it asks whether a narrow candidate basin is selected by simultaneous consistency across multiple structurally distinct diagnostics.

If the answer is yes, then 255° becomes a serious closure-selection candidate.
If the answer is no, then the framework is constrained or falsified in a useful way.

That makes this a real research problem rather than a decorative number hunt.
